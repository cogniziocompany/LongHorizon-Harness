## WHAT WAS OBSERVED, VERBATIM
`GET http://192.168.21.153:20252/api/health` on the dev lane, running `sha-20f07d1` (PR #92, merged 03:05Z), returns **HTTP 503** with:

    {"status":"unhealthy","service":"Cognizioware Billing Service","version":"sha-20f07d1",
     "databases":{"primary":"configured","secondary":"disabled","secondaryRequired":false,"error":null,
       "replicationHealth":{"pendingSecondaryAck":1,"pendingRepairQueueItems":0,
                            "failedRepairQueueItems":0,"degraded":true}},
     "dependency":null,"message":null}

Probed three times in a row: `pendingSecondaryAck` stays at **1** and never drains.

## WHY THIS IS A DEFECT AND NOT JUST A STALE COUNTER
PR #92 did exactly what was asked - `secondary: "disabled"`, `secondaryRequired: false`, and the endpoint answers in ~10ms instead of hanging. That part is correct and must NOT be undone. But the replication health check still counts records awaiting an acknowledgement from the secondary, and with the secondary disabled **nothing will ever acknowledge them**. So `degraded` is stuck true, `status` is stuck `unhealthy`, and HTTP 503 is now the service's permanent steady state. A health endpoint that can never go green is worse than no health endpoint: everything that watches it learns to ignore it.

## FIX - DECIDE THE RULE, DO NOT JUST ZERO THE COUNTER
When the secondary is disabled and not required, a pending secondary ack is **not evidence of ill health** - it is a leftover from a replication relationship that no longer exists. Establish and implement the rule: replication health must only contribute to `degraded` when the secondary is actually a participant. Read `DualDatabaseSettings`, `HealthCheckService` and whatever computes `replicationHealth` before changing anything, and say in the report which flag you keyed off and why.
Keep the counts VISIBLE in the payload - an operator should still be able to see that one record was left un-acked when the dual-write was turned off. Do not delete the field, do not silently zero it, and do NOT write to the database to drain the queue. Report the number honestly and stop letting it decide `status`.
Consider whether the leftover records deserve a one-line note in the payload (something an operator can act on later) rather than vanishing into a green light, and say what you decided.

So the lane green-lit a service that was reporting itself unhealthy. Make the rollout check assert the health STATUS as well as the sha, or say plainly why it should not.
